M2C: From Models to Collectives

Orchestration of LLMs and Agents

San Francisco, USA 29 or 30 April 2027

About the workshop

Workshop Description

Foundation model research has largely focused on improving individual models. This workshop explores a complementary paradigm: intelligence emerging from the learned coordination of heterogeneous LLMs, agents, tools, and humans.

We treat orchestration as a first-class learning problem: how should a system select collaborators, assign roles, share information, allocate computation, verify intermediate results, and synthesize a final answer?

A central question is when a collective can outperform its strongest member and be trained, evaluated, and deployed as a coherent model. By connecting multi-agent learning, LLM agents, mixture-of-experts, test-time computation, and collective intelligence, M2C aims to establish shared benchmarks, principles, and research directions for adaptive AI collectives.

Topics of Interest

Five core questions organize the workshop, with evaluation as a cross-cutting theme.

Q1

Complementary Capability

When and why can a collective solve tasks that none of its members can solve alone?

Q2

Team Formation & Routing

How should a system select models, assign roles, and decompose work for each task and budget?

Q3

Coordination & Communication

What should models share, and how should their interactions and communication be structured?

Q4

Learning to Collaborate

Which training signals can improve an orchestrator or its members as tasks and model pools change?

Q5

Efficient, Accountable Synthesis

How should collectives allocate compute, verify work, combine contributions, and attribute failures?

+

Evaluation

How should collectives be compared with their strongest member at matched compute or cost?
