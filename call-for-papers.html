Call for Papers

Contribute to M2C @ ICLR 2027.

Overview

We invite novel and ongoing work on learned collaboration among foundation models, including negative results and position papers. The workshop asks how heterogeneous models and agents can learn to collaborate as one adaptive collective—and when that collective can outperform its strongest member.

Work under review elsewhere and arXiv preprints are welcome. Work already accepted at an archival venue, including the ICLR 2027 main track, is not eligible. Submissions that claim gains from collaboration should compare against relevant baselines, including the strongest member and same-budget resampling of that member, at matched compute or cost with the unit clearly stated.

Topics of Interest

Topics include, but are not limited to:

Q1

Complementary Capability

When and why a collective beats its strongest member; measuring heterogeneity; and curating and scaling model pools.

Q2

Learned Team Formation & Routing

Learned model selection and routing, role assignment, task decomposition, and cost-aware cascades.

Q3

Coordination & Communication

Centralized and decentralized orchestration, communication topologies and protocols, emergent coordination, debate, negotiation, and robustness to faulty members.

Q4

Learning to Collaborate

Training orchestrators or members with reinforcement learning, evolution, or self-improvement; credit assignment; and adaptation to new members and tasks.

Q5

Efficient & Accountable Synthesis

Test-time compute allocation, verification, aggregation, judge reliability, failure attribution, and accountability.

+

Evaluation

Benchmarks, matched-compute or matched-cost baselines, negative results, and position papers.

Submission Tracks

Both tracks use the ICLR 2027 template, share one deadline, and receive double-blind review on OpenReview.

Full paperUp to 8 pages

Complete studies presenting substantial empirical, theoretical, or methodological work.

Tiny paperUp to 2 pages

Focused contributions for under-represented, under-resourced, and early-career researchers, and authors outside the ML publication circuit.

Page limits exclude references, appendices, and the mandatory AI-disclosure section. Tiny papers are evaluated for a clear question and sound evidence rather than completeness.

Review Criteria

Each submission will be assigned three reviewers. Reviewers will assess:

  • Relevance to the workshop’s questions
  • Technical soundness
  • Clarity of presentation
  • Potential to advance discussion
  • Appropriate matched-cost or matched-compute baselines
  • Fit to full- or tiny-paper expectations

Presentation at the Workshop

Every accepted paper will be presented as a poster. Five papers—including at least one tiny paper—will be selected for 10-minute oral presentations. Five additional tiny papers will be selected for two-minute lightning talks.

AI and LLM Use

  • Authors must disclose LLM use in the submission form and in a mandatory AI-disclosure section that does not count toward the page limit.
  • AI assistance is permitted, but submissions must be primarily human-authored and reflect original thought and analysis.
  • AI-generated papers are not accepted in either track. AI-generated tiny papers are not permitted.
  • AI systems may not be listed as authors or act as primary authors, reviewers, or meta-reviewers.
  • Authors remain fully responsible for all submitted content.
